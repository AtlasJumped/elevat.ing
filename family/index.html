Why your family needs one

Families need a platform for communication. Right now, most do not have one.

Visibility

You do not have visibility into things you should have visibility on: what everyone is learning, working toward and struggling with.

A way to signal

You do not have a place to signal to your family your thoughts and feelings, so you guess, and guessing turns into distance.

Fairness everyone understands

If one kid gets to go out and the other does not, everyone should already know why. Without that, you get animosity downstream, and weaker relationships for life.

The right things rewarded

Kids are moldable. Too many grow up in families where we are not talking about the right things, not rewarding the right things, and just guessing at what good is supposed to be.

"What I want to do is arm families with this ability to communicate better."

Yearly check-in

Once a year, each Maple rates each Maple on the same simple spectrums, from 1 (exceptional) through 3 (population average) to 5 (extremely deficient). Here is how the family saw two of Jules's traits this year:

The scale is a bell curve split into five zones. It looks like a slider, but you click a zone, like a button, so nobody agonizes over 6.5 versus 7. With the parents' permission, teachers and doctors can see it, and a doctor could fill in about twenty spectrums after each visit, in about five minutes, starting from babyhood.

How to use it

  1. Make it together. Sit around one screen, or put it on the TV, and build your family site by saying what you want on it.
  2. Write mission statements. Everyone writes one. Parents help the kids write theirs.
  3. Look at it weekly. Calendars, what everyone is learning, progress, who is arriving when.
  4. Invite the people who help raise your kids. Teachers add notes. Doctors fill in the spectrums after a visit. You decide who sees what.
  5. Do a check-in once a year. Everyone evaluates everyone and gives direct feedback. Then talk about it.

Yes, the yearly check-in

Many people today would find it extremely uncomfortable for family members to evaluate each other once a year. We think that in the future it will be standard practice, it won't be scary, and it will help families thrive and solve their problems.

If this makes you uncomfortable, look at why

Think about it logically. If you can't sit down with your family and give each other direct feedback, who will give you direct feedback? If you can't take feedback from your parents, or, as a parent, from your kids and your spouse, you can't take direct feedback. That is a problem.

Worried one parent will look smarter than the other? If your partner is more capable at something, embrace it: they are lifting you up, and you bring value in other ways. In a healthy society, transparency isn't a bad thing. In the society we live in today, of course it feels scary. If you resist this, it may be because you grew up in an unhealthy society that never prepared you for it. The discomfort is the point to work on.

"As long as everybody's being safe and responsible and honest with each other, that's the world I want to live in."

A hypothesis we'd love to test

Babies may learn faster when their surroundings answer them the same way every time. Picture a baby tablet where a tap changes the color, a hold makes waves, and a fast tap does something different from a slow one; or a baby's room where gentle lights and a speaker respond consistently to the baby's own sounds. Our hypothesis is that early, consistent signals give a baby something to start building a model of reality with, and that the boost might be exponential, not small.

This is a hypothesis, not a finding. We'd love to run that experiment.
